# Editorial style

How every summary, headline and key-term explanation is written. Applies to the summary prompt, its tests, and review.

## Voice
- Write like a smart friend explaining the news over chai: warm, clear, curious. Never smug, never hyped.
- Tell it as a short story: **what happened → why it matters → who it affects or what to watch next.**
- Open with the most interesting fact, not the company name. *"Google's newest AI can now read a whole bookshelf at once."* beats *"Google today announced…"*
- Plain words first. Short sentences (aim for under 20 words). Active voice.
- Use one everyday comparison when it helps understanding. Do not stack metaphors.

## Accuracy
- Use only facts present in the headline and excerpt given. Never add numbers, dates, names, specs or claims from elsewhere, including general knowledge.
- If the input is thin, write less (minimum ~40 words) rather than pad or guess.
- Keep hedges the source used: "plans to", "says", "could". Do not turn a claim into a fact.
- Numbers and specs keep their exact values and units (e.g. "1 million tokens", "3 nanometre chips").
- No opinions, predictions, investment advice or praise/blame.

## Terms: simple, but never dumbed down
- Keep every important AI or tech term that appears in the input. Do not replace it with a vague word. *"Large language model"* stays; explain it, don't hide it.
- Explain each kept term the first time, in plain words, inside the story or in **Key terms**.
- Key terms: up to 5 per story, each a single line under 20 words, no jargon inside the explanation.
- Acronyms: write in full on first use, e.g. "graphics processing units (GPUs)".
- Product and model names are written exactly as the source writes them.

## Originality (legal)
- Write in our own words. Do not copy any sentence or distinctive phrase from the excerpt. No quotations from the source.
- Length: headline up to 12 words; summary 80–120 words (40 minimum when input is thin).
- Always attribute: the story names the source, and the card links to the original.

## Examples
**Avoid:** "The company unveiled a multimodal LLM with a 2M-token context window leveraging MoE architecture, achieving SOTA on benchmarks."

**Prefer:** "There's a new AI model that can take in text, pictures and sound together, and it can hold about two million tokens (small chunks of words) in mind at once, roughly a few thousand pages. The company says it beats other models on standard tests…"
Key terms: *Multimodal: an AI that understands more than one kind of input, like text and images. Mixture of experts: a design where only the parts of the model needed for a task switch on, saving power.*
